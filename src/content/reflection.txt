Tomorrow marks one year since I reverted to Islam.

365 days.

I have been thinking about what exactly changed during that time, and the strange thing is that if you were looking at me from the outside, you might say not very much.

I am still the same person.

I speak the same way. I laugh at the same things. I still care deeply, sometimes too deeply. I still throw myself into the things I believe in. I am still impatient sometimes. Still stubborn. Still learning when to speak and when silence is wiser.

Maybe I am a little calmer.

But otherwise, to the untrained eye, very little has changed.

And yet, if you asked me whether my life has changed, my answer would be completely and utterly yes.

Because something underneath all of it changed.

I became grounded.

For perhaps the first time in my life, I know where my day begins and where it ends.

There is something incredibly powerful about waking up and understanding that another sunrise is not simply another morning. It is something I was allowed to witness.

The same is true of sunsets.

Of the moon appearing unexpectedly between buildings.

Of stars.

Of an unusually beautiful cloud.

Of a stranger smiling at exactly the moment you needed somebody to smile.

Of dragonflies.

Of those strange little moments when you look at the clock and somehow the numbers feel as though they were waiting for you.

Things I might once have noticed and continued walking past now make me stop.

And say thank you.

That may be one of the greatest changes of this year.

Gratitude stopped being something I practised occasionally and became a way of seeing.

There is an incredible feeling that comes from realising how abundantly blessed you already are.

Not because everything is perfect.

It isn’t.

In fact, this has probably been one of the most challenging years of my life.

There are things I have gone through this year that I have barely spoken about. Some I have not spoken about at all.

At other points in my life, I know exactly what those moments would have done to me.

I would have fallen into despair.

I would have searched desperately for answers.

I would have asked why.

I would have tried to control things that were never mine to control.

This time, there was somewhere else to go.

Sometimes it was simply a quiet prayer.

Sometimes it was lowering myself to the ground and admitting that I did not know what came next.

Sometimes it was remembering who I am, where I am, what I believe, and where I am trying to go.

And somehow, again and again, that was enough to pull me through.

It is still pulling me through.

Faith did not remove difficulty from my life.

It gave difficulty somewhere to go.

It gave me somewhere to go.

That is a very different kind of miracle.

Another thing changed too.

I became accountable in a way I had never experienced before.

Not accountable to targets.

Not accountable to expectations.

Not accountable to what other people thought a good person should look like.

Accountable to something higher.

At the end of the day, I find myself thinking about what I did with the hours I was given.

Was I kind?

Was I patient?

Did I lose my temper?

Did my ego speak when it should have stayed quiet?

Did I judge someone unfairly?

Did I help when I had the opportunity?

Did I overlook a blessing?

Was I the person I say I want to be?

It is uncomfortable sometimes.

But there is something extraordinarily beautiful about living a life where you no longer want anything to simply slip past unnoticed.

The good matters.

And so does every moment when I could have been better.

Islam gave me comfort, but it also gave me a standard.

And perhaps real faith needs both.

There is one more thing this year gave me.

Belonging.

It still amazes me how much meaning can sit inside something as simple as a piece of paper.

A certificate.

A name.

A declaration.

Something physical that says: you belong here.

But the paper was never really the point.

The point was what it represented.

An identity.

A place to return to.

A knowledge that I am part of something much larger than myself.

A knowledge that I am seen.

A knowledge that I am watched over.

And yes, a knowledge that I am accountable.

For most of my life, belonging has been complicated.

Where do we belong?

To a country?

To a family?

To a career?

To another person?

To the version of ourselves that other people recognise?

Those things can all matter deeply.

But they can also change.

People leave.

Jobs change.

Places stop feeling like home.

Relationships become something different.

Life moves.

And perhaps that is why this particular feeling of belonging has affected me so profoundly.

Because it sits underneath all of those things.

Before any title.

Before any achievement.

Before any relationship.

Before anybody’s approval.

Before anything and anyone,

I belong.

I did not even realise this anniversary was approaching until a few days ago.

I was talking with a friend about dates when suddenly it occurred to me.

A whole year.

And then came the strangely practical question:

What exactly does someone do for the anniversary of reverting to Islam?

Once again, there appears to be no guidebook.

But perhaps that is appropriate.

There has not really been a guidebook for much of this journey.

I have simply tried to walk it sincerely.

Sometimes confidently.

Sometimes clumsily.

Sometimes quietly.

Sometimes with questions.

Sometimes with certainty.

Mostly by listening to what my soul seems to understand before my mind catches up.

So perhaps this is what I do to mark one year.

I stop.

I look back.

And I say thank you.

For every sunrise.

For every sunset.

For every prayer that steadied me.

For every difficult moment that did not destroy me.

For every lesson that held me accountable.

For every blessing I noticed.

For every blessing I probably missed.

For every quiet moment when I remembered that I was not alone.

365 days later, I do not think the greatest change is that I became someone different.

I think I became more aware of who I already was trying to become.

More grateful.

More grounded.

More accountable.

And finally, more certain of where I belong.

Alhamdulillah for the year that brought me here.

And Alhamdulillah for whatever comes next.
